1. SYSTEM UNDERSTANDING & DOMAIN CONTEXT
================================================================================

1.1 DOMAIN BACKGROUND & MOTIVATION
Traditional university libraries are rich repositories of knowledge, housing 
tens of thousands of physical books, quiet study desks, and collaborative group 
study rooms. However, the software systems running these institutions 
(legacy Online Public Access Catalogs or OPACs) were designed decades ago. 
They act as static, rigid databases rather than intelligent user-centric tools.

Students frequently experience cognitive overload, physical disorientation, and 
wasted study time when interacting with multi-floor physical library buildings. 
LibraMind was conceived to bridge the gap between digital discovery and the 
physical building layout by blending modern artificial intelligence, 
concurrency-safe space booking, and precise spatial coordinate mapping.

1.2 PROBLEM FORMULATION & REAL-WORLD BOTTLENECKS
Through empirical observation of academic library operations, three primary 
systemic bottlenecks were identified:

  1. Physical Stack Confusion (Search-to-Hand Friction):
     - Symptom: OPACs provide an abstract Dewey Decimal number (e.g., 005.133 S55).
     - Impact: Students wander through multiple floors, sections, and blind aisles 
       searching for a book, losing 15 to 25 minutes per visit or giving up.
     - Root Cause: Absence of granular spatial coordinates linked to the catalog.

  2. Study Space Friction & Double-Booking Conflicts:
     - Symptom: During peak exam periods, study pods fill rapidly. Students walk 
       across campus only to find zero desks available.
     - Impact: Disputes arise when two students claim the same desk, or when 
       students hold desks without formal reservations.
     - Root Cause: Lack of synchronized, real-time, concurrency-safe seat booking.

  3. Rigid Keyword Catalog Search:
     - Symptom: Basic SQL "LIKE" queries fail when students do not know exact 
       titles, ISBNs, or author spellings (e.g., searching "scalable cloud backends").
     - Impact: High-value academic books remain undiscovered on shelves.
     - Root Cause: Lack of natural language processing and semantic vector mapping.

1.3 STAKEHOLDER ANALYSIS & USER PERSONAS
LibraMind serves three distinct user classes, each with specialized needs:

  A. Undergraduate & Postgraduate Students (Patrons):
     - Needs: Quick discovery of relevant research titles, instant physical 
       location tags, stress-free study pod booking, and clear loan due countdowns.
     - Key Workflows: Search by research intent, add to Visit Planner, reserve 
       study carrel, view queue position, and renew loans online.

  B. Desk Librarians (Operations):
     - Needs: Rapid circulation processing, individual copy barcode scanning, 
       condition tracking, hold queue staging, and broadcast announcements.
     - Key Workflows: Check out book by barcode, mark returned copies, stage 
       ready-for-pickup holds, and inspect damaged copies.

  C. Library Directors & Administrators (Management & Governance):
     - Needs: Campus-wide circulation analytics, user permission elevation, 
       catalog data exports, audit trail inspection, and demand forecasting.
     - Key Workflows: Monitor seat occupancy density, forecast inventory 
       stockouts for budget planning, and review system integrity logs.

1.4 COGNITIVE ERGONOMICS & BIOPHILIC DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
Library software is often clinical, stark, and stressful. LibraMind adopts 
Biophilic Design principles:
  - Color Palette: Deep Forest Green (#344E41), Sage (#829779), Botanical (#52734D), 
    and Soft Cream (#FAF8F1).
  - Visual Ergonomics: Eliminates stark #FFFFFF glare, reducing eye fatigue 
    during late-night exam study sessions.
  - Typography: Pairs a contemporary geometric sans-serif (Outfit) for clean 
    data legibility with an editorial serif (Newsreader) for literary elegance.
  - Micro-Interactions: 200ms cubic-bezier transitions provide organic, tactile 
    feedback without lag or distraction.

2. FEASIBILITY ANALYSIS
================================================================================

2.1 TECHNICAL FEASIBILITY (ASSESSMENT: HIGHLY FEASIBLE)
  - Frontend: React 19 combined with Vite 8 delivers an ultra-fast Single Page 
    Application (SPA) with lightning-fast Hot Module Replacement and production 
    bundles under 400KB.
  - Backend: Node.js and Express 5 provide an event-driven, asynchronous I/O 
    runtime ideal for handling hundreds of concurrent REST queries.
  - Database: SQLite 3 via `better-sqlite3` operating with Write-Ahead Logging 
    (WAL) mode delivers high-performance in-process ACID transactions without the 
    network latency or operational maintenance of external database servers.
  - Natural Language Processing: In-memory TF-IDF vectorization executes in under 
    15 milliseconds without requiring expensive external cloud GPU APIs.

2.2 OPERATIONAL FEASIBILITY (ASSESSMENT: HIGHLY FEASIBLE)
  - The system requires zero specialized client installation; it operates inside 
    any modern web browser on desktop, tablet, or mobile devices.
  - 1-Click demo accounts and role-based views ensure minimal onboarding friction 
    for students, librarians, and administrative staff.

2.3 ECONOMIC & RESOURCE FEASIBILITY (ASSESSMENT: HIGHLY COST-EFFECTIVE)
  - Built entirely using open-source technologies (MIT/Apache licenses).
  - Operates on modest hardware infrastructure (can run efficiently on a single 
    standard virtual machine or local campus server).
  - Zero ongoing per-query API subscription costs for search or AI recommendations.

2.4 SCHEDULE & IMPLEMENTATION FEASIBILITY (ASSESSMENT: COMPLETED & VERIFIED)
  - Modular decoupled architecture enabled independent frontend and backend 
    development, verified with an automated 12-scenario integration test suite.

3. REQUIREMENT ANALYSIS
================================================================================

3.1 FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS SPECIFICATION (FRS)

[FRS-1] AI Discovery & Natural Language Search Engine
  - 1.1 The system shall parse natural language search queries, stripping common 
        English stopwords and punctuation.
  - 1.2 The system shall compute TF-IDF vector embeddings over book titles, 
        authors, categories, and descriptive summaries.
  - 1.3 The system shall compute Cosine Similarity scores and return ranked 
        matches with a minimum similarity threshold (> 0.05).
  - 1.4 The system shall display transparent match explanations indicating the 
        exact keyword tokens that triggered each recommendation.
  - 1.5 The system shall capture student research interests upon registration and 
        dynamically generate a personalized "Recommended for You" carousel.
  - 1.6 The system shall provide an in-app AI conversational assistant to answer 
        catalog, hours, and library policy inquiries.

[FRS-2] 4-Tier Spatial Coordinate Hierarchy & Barcode Tracking
  - 2.1 The system shall index every cataloged title with exact physical spatial 
        coordinates: Floor, Section, Shelf Code, and Rack Unit.
  - 2.2 The system shall assign and track individual physical copies using unique 
        barcodes (e.g., LM-CS-01-C1).
  - 2.3 The system shall maintain real-time copy states: 'available', 'borrowed', 
        'reserved', and 'maintenance'.
  - 2.4 The system shall support a "My Library Visit Planner" allowing patrons 
        to add books to an itinerary, auto-grouped by floor and section.

[FRS-3] Study Pod Management & Concurrency-Safe Booking
  - 3.1 The system shall maintain an interactive floor-by-floor map of study pods 
        categorized into Silent Focus, Collaborative Labs, and Window Nooks.
  - 3.2 The system shall enforce date and time-slot reservation windows.
  - 3.3 The system shall strictly prevent double-booking the same seat, date, and 
        time slot under concurrent multi-user access.
  - 3.4 The system shall provide librarians with real-time override toggles 
        ('available', 'occupied', 'maintenance').

[FRS-4] Circulation Engine, Fines & FIFO Hold Queues
  - 4.1 The system shall enforce a standard 14-day borrowing duration per loan.
  - 4.2 The system shall enforce a maximum limit of 5 active loans per student.
  - 4.3 The system shall calculate automated overdue fines at $1.00 per day for 
        any loan exceeding its due date.
  - 4.4 The system shall allow self-service 1-click renewals (up to 2 times), 
        provided there are no pending hold reservations on the title.
  - 4.5 When available copies reach zero, patrons shall be placed into an 
        ordered FIFO waitlist queue with visible queue positions.
  - 4.6 When a borrowed book is returned, the system shall automatically mark a 
        copy as "reserved" for Queue Position #1 and dispatch an immediate 
        pickup alert with a 48-hour deadline.

[FRS-5] Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) & Administrative Workspaces
  - 5.1 The system shall support three distinct security roles: Student, Librarian, 
        and Administrator, authenticated via JSON Web Tokens (JWT) and bcryptjs.
  - 5.2 Students shall have read access to catalog and seats, and read/write 
        access only to their own loans, holds, and bookings.
  - 5.3 Librarians shall have desk-level management privileges to check out, check 
        in, stage holds, and broadcast notices.
  - 5.4 Administrators shall possess full system rights, including role changes, 
        full catalog CSV exports, and immutable audit log reviews.

[FRS-6] Analytics & Predictive Demand Forecasting
  - 6.1 The system shall compute real-time statistics: Total Titles, Active Loans, 
    Overdue Count, Seat Occupancy Percentage, and Category Distributions.
  - 6.2 The system shall generate a 30-Day Predictive Demand Index for each 
        category based on circulation velocity, hold depth, and inventory ratios.

3.2 NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS SPECIFICATION (NFRS)

[NFRS-1] Latency & Performance:
  - Catalog searches and TF-IDF similarity queries must resolve in < 50ms.
  - Seat availability matrix rendering must resolve in < 30ms.
  - Frontend initial bundle size must remain under 500KB gzipped.

[NFRS-2] Concurrency & Transactional Atomicity (ACID):
  - All inventory modifications (decrementing available copies and issuing loan 
    records) must execute within atomic database transactions.
  - Zero double-booking tolerance for study pods under concurrent web requests.

[NFRS-3] Security & Data Integrity:
  - Passwords must be hashed using bcrypt with salt rounds >= 10.
  - All API routes handling user-specific mutations must validate JWT signatures.
  - Foreign key constraints (ON DELETE CASCADE / SET NULL) must be enforced 
    at the database engine level via SQLite PRAGMA.

[NFRS-4] Availability & Reliability:
  - Database must operate in Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) mode to allow concurrent 
    readers while writes are in progress without database locking errors.
  - Centralized error-handling middleware must return sanitized error payloads.

[NFRS-5] Ergonomics & Accessibility:
  - Contrast ratios must comply with WCAG 2.1 AA standards.
  - Responsive layouts must dynamically adjust from 320px mobile displays to 
    4K desktop monitors.

4.3 CONCURRENCY CONTROL ANALYSIS (ZERO DOUBLE-BOOKING GUARANTEE)
A pivotal engineering achievement in LibraMind is the elimination of race 
conditions when reserving study pods. 

Traditional applications rely on application-level checks:
  1. `SELECT * FROM seat_reservations WHERE seat_id = 1 AND slot = '10:00 AM'`
  2. If empty, `INSERT INTO seat_reservations ...`
Under high concurrent loads (e.g., 50 students clicking simultaneously at 9:00 AM), 
both requests execute step 1 before either executes step 2, causing double-bookings.

LibraMind implements an atomic database-level constraint:
  CREATE UNIQUE INDEX idx_seat_active_reservation 
  ON seat_reservations (seat_id, date, time_slot) 
  WHERE status = 'active';

When two concurrent requests hit the SQLite engine:
  - Request A acquires the WAL write lock, inserts the row, and succeeds.
  - Request B immediately throws an `SQLITE_CONSTRAINT_UNIQUE` violation.
  - The backend catches the exception cleanly and returns an informative HTTP 409 
    Conflict message ("This study pod has just been reserved by another student").

5. ALGORITHMIC & MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS
================================================================================

5.1 TF-IDF VECTOR SPACE MODEL & COSINE SIMILARITY ENGINE
To understand and match natural language user queries with library catalog 
records, LibraMind utilizes an in-memory vector space model.

A. Tokenization & Weighted Document Construction:
   For each book document D_i, a weighted token sequence is constructed:
     Tokens(D_i) = 3*(Title) + 2*(Category) + 2*(Author) + 1*(Description)

B. Term Frequency (TF):
   TF(t, D_i) = (Count of term t in D_i) / (Total terms in D_i)

C. Inverse Document Frequency (IDF):
   IDF(t) = ln( 1 + (N / (1 + DF(t))) )
   where N = Total catalog titles, and DF(t) = number of documents containing t.

D. TF-IDF Score Vector:
   V(t, D_i) = TF(t, D_i) * IDF(t)

E. Cosine Similarity Function:
   Given user query vector Q and book document vector D_i:
   
                    Q • D_i                   ∑ ( Q_t * D_i,t )
   CosineSim(Q, D_i) = ---------- = ----------------------------------------
                    ||Q||*||D_i||   sqrt( ∑ (Q_t)^2 ) * sqrt( ∑ (D_i,t)^2 )

   - The resulting coefficient ranges from 0.0 (no relevance) to 1.0 (exact match).
   - Any score >= 0.05 is ranked, sorted descending, and augmented with 
     transparent keyword match tags for Explainable AI (XAI).

5.2 AUTOMATED OVERDUE & FINE CALCULATION LOGIC
For all active loans where CurrentTime > DueDate:
  - Overdue Days = ceil( (CurrentTime - DueDate) / (1000 * 60 * 60 * 24) )
  - Fine Amount  = Overdue Days * $1.00

5.3 30-DAY PREDICTIVE DEMAND FORECASTING FORMULATION
To assist administrators in procurement and stock allocation, a predictive 
demand index (D_index) from 0 to 100 is formulated for each subject category:

  D_index = min(100, round( (C_active * W_c + R_wait * W_r + B_total * W_b) / K ))

  Where:
    - C_active = Active & overdue loans in category (Weight W_c = 18)
    - R_wait   = Active waitlist reservations in category (Weight W_r = 25)
    - B_total  = Total physical copies owned (Weight W_b = 5)
    - K        = Normalizing scaling factor (1.5)

  If D_index > 60: Projected 30-day demand growth is classified as HIGH (+24%).
  If D_index <= 60: Projected 30-day demand growth is classified as MODERATE (+12%).

7. RISK ASSESSMENT & MITIGATION STRATEGIES
================================================================================

1. Risk: High-concurrency seat reservation race condition.
   - Severity: High.
   - Mitigation: Enforced atomic Partial Unique Index in SQLite schema.

2. Risk: Patron holds a study pod but does not arrive (No-Show).
   - Severity: Medium.
   - Mitigation: Time-slot bounding combined with librarian one-click status 
     overrides ('available', 'maintenance').

3. Risk: Database read/write contention under high patron traffic.
   - Severity: Medium.
   - Mitigation: Enabled SQLite Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) mode, allowing concurrent 
     reads while writes are executing.

4. Risk: High-demand titles causing patron dissatisfaction due to long queues.
   - Severity: Low to Medium.
   - Mitigation: Automated FIFO queue transparency showing exact queue position, 
     accompanied by the 30-day demand forecast alerting staff to buy copies.

5. Risk: Security vulnerability through client parameter tampering.
   - Severity: High.
   - Mitigation: Strict server-side verification using signed JWT tokens and 
     role-check middleware (`requireRole(['admin', 'librarian'])`).
